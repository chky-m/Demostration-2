Explore television energy use

The course data records television models, their screen sizes, star ratings and labelled annual energy consumption. Explore the charts to compare the records, while remembering that model counts are not sales figures.

What you can explore

Reading a TV energy label

The raw TV file contains Star2 and a labelled energy figure in kWh/year. The course dictionary identifies Star2 as the actual star rating and describes comparative energy consumption as nominal yearly use. A rating and an energy amount answer different questions.

Sources: Week 1 context, COS30045 W1.2 Data Processing.pdf (slide 27); raw file tv_2026_02_15.csv; course data dictionary televisionsmay2015(1).docx.

Comparing similar screens

The 55-inch records make a more focused comparison. Their mean labelled consumption is 327.89 kWh/year for LCD, 370.76 for LED and 351.72 for OLED. These are averages of model records, with different numbers of models in each group.

Source: Week 6, Ex6_TVdata_withStar.csv; calculated by data/prepare_data.py.

Australian market context

The Week 1 exercise frames these records as televisions available on the Australian market. The raw data's SoldIn field lists countries where a model is registered for sale. Registrations describe model availability, not how many televisions people bought.

Sources: Week 1, COS30045 W1.2 Data Processing.pdf (slide 27); tv_2026_02_15.csv and its course data dictionary.
